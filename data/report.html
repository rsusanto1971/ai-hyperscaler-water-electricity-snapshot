The OpenAI gap

OpenAI is not comparable to the other four companies in this table, and that gap is itself the finding. Google, Microsoft, Amazon, and Meta each publish an annual sustainability report with a defined reporting boundary, a named methodology, and in most cases outside accountant review. OpenAI publishes none of this. Its only public figure, roughly 0.000085 gallons and 0.34 watt-hours per ChatGPT query, comes from a June 2025 personal blog post by Sam Altman, not a company report. OpenAI has never defined what an "average query" is, never named which model the estimate covers, and never released a methodology behind it. In September 2026, Altman made a second public claim on a podcast, that 38,000 ChatGPT queries use the same water as growing one California almond, which PolitiFact rated "Mostly False" within days. A company operating some of the largest AI compute buildouts in the world, including the Stargate data center program, has disclosed less about its resource use than any traditional cloud provider discloses about a single one of its buildings.

Methodology

Each company was scored 1 to 5 in three categories, weighted equally, for a total out of 15. Scores are relative to the other four companies in this set, not to an absolute external standard, since no independent, audited standard for AI data center water disclosure currently exists.

Weight: 1 / 3

Water efficiency

Based on Water Usage Effectiveness (L/kWh) where disclosed. A company with no fleet-wide WUE cannot be verified as efficient, regardless of its other numbers, and is scored accordingly.

Weight: 1 / 3

Disclosure quality

Whether the company publishes a named methodology, third-party review, and, most importantly, whether it accounts for indirect water used to generate its purchased electricity, not just on-site cooling.

Weight: 1 / 3

Trend direction

Whether usage or intensity is improving or worsening year over year, and whether that direction, good or bad, is reported plainly rather than buried or omitted.

Two things affected scores across the board. First, none of the five companies publish a verified, methodology-backed water or energy figure tied to a specific AI workload, query, or model. Google's per-prompt Gemini figure is the closest thing to an exception, and it is a separate technical blog disclosure, not part of its audited environmental report. Second, only Meta quantifies indirect water, the water used by power plants to generate the electricity these companies buy. Google, Microsoft, and Amazon all report on-site water only; Amazon at least acknowledges the gap in writing, Google and Microsoft do not address it.

Sources are each company's own most recent sustainability or environmental report, plus, where a company's own site could not be reached directly from this environment, closely corroborating secondary reporting from outlets covering the same disclosure (cross-checked across multiple independent sources before being used). No paid data provider (MSCI, Sustainalytics, Bloomberg, Refinitiv) was used.
